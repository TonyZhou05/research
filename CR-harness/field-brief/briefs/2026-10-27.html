1. ThinkJev watch

What it is. arXiv:2610.37732 (submitted 27 Oct 2026). System 1 reads option-letter probabilities from a direct answer; System 2 reads the same letters after a thought on the same cached prompt, so escalating repeats no work. System 1 answers alone only where a statistical test on labelled dev data guarantees accepted answers stay under a target error rate.

Evidence. At a 5% target, Qwen3.5-27B answers 24.5% of 8,359 test decisions directly with 3.9% realised error (4B and 9B: 6.5% and 0.0%). Early-stopping each thought at confidence reaches 81.6% accuracy vs 82.7% for always thinking, using 72% of thought tokens. Escalating on System 1 confidence alone saves less, because the decisions it is least sure of are often not the ones thinking fixes. JevBench comparison is labelled exploratory by the authors.

Why it matters for CR. A sharper version of our Jev router gate: certify the fast path per decision type with a held-out error bound, rather than a raw confidence threshold.

Suggested next step

  • On logged CR decision_points, compare a confidence threshold against a dev-set error-bound accept rule at a 5% target.
