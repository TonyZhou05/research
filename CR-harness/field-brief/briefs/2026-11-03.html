Field Brief — pick from the menu, and make the order not matter

Tuesday edition. Five new watch cards from public arXiv items submitted 30 Oct–2 Nov: RL directly over a labeled action menu, order-invariant typed choices, runtime harness self-editing on an open-world game, rule compliance after context compaction, and decision-model policy gates for coding agents.

Honesty. Written 3 Nov 2026 from one scout of the arXiv cs.AI/cs.LG/cs.CL/cs.MA new-submission lists. I read abstracts only and quote the authors’ numbers without re-running them. No cards retired. No candidate_intent added or dropped. Board: 17 cards (8 candidate_intent + 9 watch).

1 · RLiD: train on the action menu, not the text

watch arXiv:2611.02455. Observation, compact memory and a labeled action menu go in; a decision model picks an option in one forward pass, so there is no action parsing and no invalid output. On ALFWorld the train-and-distill pipeline reaches 99.3% success in 12.7 H100 GPU-hours vs 86.6% in 17.3 for token-level RL of the same LLM; with compiled memory a 1.5B model hits 100% on all 274 seen and unseen games.

For CR: this is the decision_point over legal_actions, trained directly. It argues for logging menu-index actions in the ETL and gives the Jev router cards a training path, not just a zero-shot one.

2 · IJev: zero flips under option reordering

watch arXiv:2611.01015. Typed one-pass choosers depend on option order and on a prior over answer labels. Iterated cyclic reordering with label-prior correction is exactly order-invariant; flip rate is zero in all 25 benchmark×model cells and calibration improves over a single cyclic average. A same-week audit (arXiv:2611.00496) shows option swaps flipping 0–72.6% of choices across setups.

For CR: hand slots and tile indices are arbitrary orderings. Any selector over legal_actions should ship a permutation flip-rate metric before its probabilities feed calibration or abstention.

3 · Exo: let the harness change, keep the mechanism frozen

watch arXiv:2611.00463. The agent edits its own harness and strategies as inspectable code during work; a small protected layer keeps execution history and controls which policy is active. On long-running open-world-game objectives: 12–53% lower model cost than Codex and Pi standalone, 45–82% in transfer runs, at matched completion; 35–61% lower held-out cost on AppWorld and SREGym vs the same policy without editing.

For CR: draw the line before self-editing arrives: action validation, logging and policy activation stay frozen; strategy code is fair game.

4 · ARCBench: the rule survives compaction; compliance doesn’t

watch arXiv:2611.03223. Built on the open-source pi harness. Most violations after compaction happen while the rule is still in the summary, with the agent reading a later request as permission. Runs that keep the rule still violate more than twice as often as full-history runs; a compactor-targeted attack more than doubles violations.

For CR: hard constraints (legal-move checks, repo guardrails) belong in harness code, not only in context that will be summarized.

5 · Policy gates: write the boundary as examples

watch arXiv:2611.02155 · dataset/rules. 2,643 synthetic tool calls, 28 rules, sealed holdout, 22 decision models + 2 prompted LLMs as gate classifiers. Example/counterexample boundaries raised recall 5.5–26.1 points on all 23 available models and cut false positives on 21; Jev 1.14 went from 89.3% to 98.2% recall and from 30.6% to 4.8% unnecessary interruptions, at a 120 ms median.

For CR: a concrete recipe and two metrics (recall, false-interrupt rate) for the gate pattern already on the board.
