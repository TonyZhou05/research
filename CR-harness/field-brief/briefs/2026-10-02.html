1. OmniMed-Jev: a distribution over the candidate set watch

What it is. Tang, Chen et al., arXiv:2610.00381 (submitted 30 Sep 2026; code). A second paper built on Jev after REFLEX (09-25 edition). Each bounded decision is a Choice, Noul or Score over a runtime-supplied candidate set, and the model returns a full probability distribution over that set rather than generated text.

Evidence. In an interface-controlled comparison with a generative baseline trained on the same backbone, data and schedule, the reported probabilities track observed correctness far more closely: calibration error down by up to an order of magnitude and reliability error by up to two, with comparable point-prediction performance. Counting is the one family where the generative baseline stays ahead. The domain is medical imaging, and the authors say the comparison can’t separate the interface from associated training differences and is not evidence of clinical readiness.

Why it matters for CR. It supports the calibration half of the Jev-at-decision_point idea: a distribution over the runtime legal_actions set is a better-behaved confidence signal than a probability read off generated text. It doesn’t show that on Mini Royale.

Suggested next step

  • When the Jev router is wired in, log the full distribution over legal ids and check calibration by match phase (see the 09-28 TC-ECE card) before trusting a confidence floor.

2. Finding the Right Fit: rankings reverse across harnesses watch

What it is. Li, Zhou, Teng et al., arXiv:2610.00917 (1 Oct 2026; adapters + 6,204 scored trajectories). 66 model–harness configurations (four configurable harnesses with five models, plus native vendor pairings) on TUA-Bench, ALE-CLI and Terminal-Bench 4.

Evidence. Model rankings reverse across harnesses: on Terminal-Bench 4 Claude leads GPT by 7.94 points in OpenHands but trails by 30.16 in PI. For four of five models the best harness changes between benchmarks. A model’s own vendor harness is not reliably its best, and higher cost doesn’t reliably buy a higher score (GPT scores higher under PI than under DSH at under a quarter of the cost per task). The matched trajectories suggest fit depends on whether the harness hands failures back in a form the model can use.

Why it matters for CR. The strongest public backing so far for reporting CR results per (model, prompt, legal_actions schema, retry policy) rather than per model. It also says the feedback given on a rejected action is part of the configuration.

Suggested next step

  • Run a small model × harness-policy grid on Mini Royale (list shown or not, rejection feedback format, retry count) and report cost per match next to win rate. It replaces the 09-24 Harness-Bench card with a stronger result.

3. Is the retry rescuing or disrupting? watch

What it is. Xiao, Wang, Chen et al., arXiv:2610.00372 (30 Sep 2026). Harness recovery is framed as a causal decision: from the same execution state, compare with and without recovery, separate rescue from harm, and track how its value changes over time. A Causal Intervention Router (CIR) uses pre-recovery information to decide when to intervene.

Evidence. On long-horizon ALFWorld with Qwen3-14B, success goes from 70.33% to 73.33% (+3.00 points) and trajectories with correct observations are left untouched. One benchmark and one 14B model, so treat the size of the gain as indicative only.

Why it matters for CR. CR’s reject-and-retry loop is a recovery mechanism, and the same retry can rescue or hurt. Mini Royale’s sim can branch from a saved decision_point, which makes the with/without comparison cheap.

Suggested next step

  • From saved decision_point states that triggered a rejection, branch with and without the retry and report rescue and harm rates instead of average win rate.

4. Weak reviewers need grounding watch

What it is. Guo, Gu, Jin et al., arXiv:2610.01023 (1 Oct 2026). 411 execution-labeled traces from three coding agents and 101 controlled cases, asking when a weaker reviewer can decide whether a patch solves its issue.

Evidence. Structured but unchecked evidence raises both defect catch and over-rejection. With official execution evidence (an upper-bound diagnostic), five of six reviewers improve both rates and two classify every held-out trace correctly; reviewer size is not a consistent predictor. A deployable cascade (patch-caused static errors plus generated tests that first fail on the unpatched repo) gets coverage 0.89/0.86 and catch 0.76/0.80 on GPT-5.4/Gemini traces, but over-rejection of 0.66/0.67. Reliable checks without official tests are still the bottleneck.

Why it matters for CR. For the second dual-LGTM seat: ground the review in executed checks, not the author’s summary, and expect generated checks to over-reject, so the human merge gate stays.

Suggested next step

  • For CR cockpit/eval PRs, ask the reviewer seat to attach a failing-before/passing-after check or a sim replay, and track how often its blocking findings were later overturned.
