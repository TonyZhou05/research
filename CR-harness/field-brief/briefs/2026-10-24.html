1. JevAgentHarness: fitted confidence thresholds watch

What it is. arXiv:2610.21412 (submitted 15 Oct 2026). AgentDecisionBench evaluates decision models from isolated questions up to control of a running agent, on public benchmarks and a DecisionGym environment; JevAgentHarness fits a per-choice confidence threshold on the agent’s own trajectories and hands decisions below it to the LLM.

Evidence. The most accurate model on isolated questions solved only 69.5% of agent tasks, and delegating tool choices without a fitted threshold lowered success by 19.0 points. An open AgentDecision-4B trained on post-mistake states (Jev labels) solved 91.5% of out-of-distribution tasks vs 82.0% for Jev; in-distribution, 4B models made agents 81.1–89.0% cheaper and up to 2.5× faster without losing 3+ points. Code release is announced but I have not verified it.

Why it matters for CR. Closest result yet to our Jev router: isolated accuracy is not closed-loop success, and the threshold must be fitted per decision type.

Suggested next step

  • Log CR decision_points with Jev confidence and outcome; fit one threshold per decision type and compare against a single global threshold.

2. Feedback that backfires in games watch

What it is. arXiv:2610.30205 (submitted 21 Oct 2026). Tests whether writing a failed move and its error back into the transcript helps small LLM players in Sokoban, Connect Four and Othello.

Evidence. Pooled over 8 instruction-tuned models (135M–1.7B), the record makes the failed move more likely (−1.38 nats/token), for illegal moves but not losing ones. A “do not repeat” instruction does nothing, a clean retry is the worst harness, a decoder ban stops exact repeats but not illegal play, and describing the failure without the move string preserves the model’s preference. Small models only.

Why it matters for CR. Our retry-on-illegal path is exactly this pattern; masking to legal_actions is the safer default.

Suggested next step

  • Ablate the CR retry message: with vs without the move string, vs masking, on the small-model tier.

3. Agents’ Last Doubt (SURE-Act) watch

What it is. arXiv:2610.24194 (submitted 16 Oct 2026). A counterfactual ToolSandbox dataset: 3,115 action outcomes from 1,036 decision points, where every legal action is executed and scored on completion, interaction cost and irreversible errors. SURE-Act ranks the alternatives for a frozen backbone.

Evidence. +0.0309 offline action-selection utility over regression-only; up to +0.0575 ToolSandbox Overall with Qwen3-4B; beats adapted SAGE-Agent and Calibrate-Then-Act rules by 0.1506 and 0.1438. Absolute gains are modest.

Why it matters for CR. A concrete way to label act vs escalate at a decision_point: branch all legal actions from saved states.

Suggested next step

  • Snapshot ~100 CR states, roll out each legal action, and use outcomes as gate labels.

4. TypedBench watch

What it is. arXiv:2610.11392 (submitted 8 Oct 2026; listed as also-seen on 10-10). Benchmark for typed decision models like Jev: seven policy-labelled generators, nine suites, accuracy as median/range across paraphrases, calibration vs a finite-sample noise floor, selective prediction and realised cost under asymmetric costs.

Evidence. The hosted model follows the stated policy but is wording-sensitive and systematically underconfident; under asymmetric costs, using its probabilities can be worse than its top answer. Open decoders (0.8B–9B) are least accurate on policy questions and degrade with more options.

Why it matters for CR. Pairs with card 1: before thresholding Jev probabilities, check paraphrase robustness and that cost-weighted choice beats argmax.

Suggested next step

  • Paraphrase CR option labels 3 ways and measure answer flips and calibration.

Also seen (not carded)

  • Can LLMs improve their own harness? (ARC-AGI-3) (21 Oct): across 27 lineages, two self-revisions raise mean held-out RHAE by only 2.80 points (95% CI −1.44 to 7.35); dev gains overstate held-out improvement.
  • RealtimeBench / TORSI (14 Oct): 180 live-clock game tasks; a slow teacher writes instructions for a fast student, raising Jev from 13.51 to 24.19.
  • System1-Arena (15 Oct): 92k questions and 138 closed-loop environments; large System-1 models match fast closed LLMs at a quarter of the latency, but small ones rarely succeed in closed loops.
