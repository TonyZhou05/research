1 · LOLPlay: more reasoning effort, fewer rounds, no gain

watch arXiv:2611.03685. 36 champion-specific tasks in native League of Legends, played through screenshots and mouse/keyboard, with 82 executable subgoal checks so partial progress is scored separately from completion. Over 324 task evaluations (three models, three effort settings), GPT-6 Astra leads with 25.0% task success at low and medium effort and 40.3% subgoal success at medium. High effort improved neither metric over low effort for any model; for Astra, longer requests meant fewer interaction rounds. Failure traces show subgoals not being composed and actions landing after the scene had already changed.

For CR: this lines up with the real-time cards already on the board: in a game that does not pause, per-call deliberation is paid for in missed decision points. The executable subgoal checks are the more reusable idea, a cheap way to score CR eval runs on partial progress (held a lane, made a positive trade) instead of only win or loss.

2 · KPHarness: probe, then keep only revisions that win a paired eval

watch arXiv:2610.27417 (project page). The model (a frozen vision-language-action policy on Minecraft) is never updated; only its execution harness changes. Recovery knowledge proposes what intervention might help, active probes test when it applies, and a slow research loop turns the evidence into bounded harness revisions. Each revision is run against the incumbent under matched sandbox conditions and accepted only when the paired evaluation improves. The authors report better coverage than bare execution, a hand-written harness and knowledge-only evolution, and better completion on unseen tasks with everything frozen.

For CR: the accept-only-on-paired-improvement rule is the gate CR-Harness would want before any self-revised rule ships, and it fits the existing ETL replays. Pairs with autoharness-textarena and the 11-03 self-revision card.
